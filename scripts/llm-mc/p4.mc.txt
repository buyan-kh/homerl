=== llm-tf-19
W A dense Transformer forward is about 2N FLOPs per token (each param is used in a multiply-add). Backward is about twice that, 4N, so training is 6N per token. Inference is just the forward, 2N, plus whatever the kernel overhead is. The 6N rule is the usual planning formula.

=== llm-pt-05

=== llm-pt-07

=== llm-pt-10

=== llm-pt-11

=== llm-pt-12

=== llm-pt-13

=== llm-pt-14

=== llm-p4-03
G fsdp-shards
W In FSDP / ZeRO-3, before a unit's forward you all-gather its parameters, then you can free them; in backward you all-gather again, compute grads, and reduce-scatter the grads so each rank keeps its shard. The optimizer step is local. Traffic is about 1.5x plain DP in the usual accounting, in exchange for much lower parameter memory.
+ All-gather params for forward and again for backward, reduce-scatter grads, then a local optimizer step. About 1.5x DP traffic, much less param memory.
- Parameters never move, and only the loss is broadcast. Traffic is 0 compared with DP.
- FSDP is 3 all-reduces of the full model per step, about 10x DP traffic, with no memory win.

=== llm-pt-15

=== llm-pt-16

=== llm-pt-18

=== llm-in-01
G softmax-temperature
W You divide logits by T before softmax. For [2, 1, 0] at T=1, softmax is unchanged from the raw logits. At T=0.5 the values become [4, 2, 0], which is sharper. High T flattens. The ranking of the logits does not change, only the sharpness.
+ Divide logits by T. T=0.5 sharpens [2,1,0] (they become [4,2,0] before softmax); T=1 leaves them as is; high T flattens. Ranking is unchanged.
- T=0.5 flattens the distribution and T=1.0 reverses the ranking of [2,1,0].
- Temperature adds T to each logit, so T=0.5 turns [2,1,0] into [2.5, 1.5, 0.5] and can change the top token.
- Temperature is applied after sampling, so the probabilities stay the same at every T.

=== llm-in-02

- top-p=0.8 keeps the top 80% of the vocab by count, here four of five tokens, including 0.05.
- After top-p the surviving probabilities stay [0.5, 0.2, 0.15] and are not renormalized.

=== llm-tf-20

=== llm-in-07

=== llm-in-08

=== llm-in-10

=== llm-in-11

=== llm-in-16

- Both styles are identical at runtime, and you should always quantize activations first, then weights.
- You never use weight-only for decode, because decode is compute bound.

=== llm-in-17
W GPTQ approximates a layer's output and uses second-order (Hessian) information to compensate weight errors as it quantizes. + GPTQ compensates quantization error with second-order information. AWQ protects salient channels by scaling them.
- GPTQ and AWQ are training-time optimizers, like AdamW, and they do not quantize weights.
- AWQ uses a Hessian, and GPTQ only scales channels, the reverse of the usual story.
- Both methods randomly drop 50% of channels, which is the "salient" step.

=== llm-in-18

- Activation outliers only exist in fp32 training, not in inference, so int8 is always safe.
- The only fix is to drop int8 and never use finer-grained scales.

=== llm-in-19

=== llm-in-20
W If each token is accepted with probability a=0.8, and you draft k=4 plus maybe a bonus corrected token, the usual geometric formula for tokens per target pass is (1 - a^(k+1)) / (1 - a) = (1 - 0.8^5) / 0.2 = 3.361. You do not get k tokens every time, because rejections stop the block.

=== llm-in-23

=== llm-in-26
